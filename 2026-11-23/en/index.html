COP31 ends without a deal: the longest climate summit ever fails in Antalya over fossil fuels

Fact confirmed: final text not adopted on Sunday · UN says it is «disappointed» · work moves on to COP32, in Ethiopia · 22/11/2026

There was no deal. COP31, the UN climate conference in Antalya, Turkey, ended on Sunday 22 November without the almost 200 countries approving the final text. The summit was due to close on Friday and ran on for around 48 hours — the longest ever, according to AFP. The COP31 president, Turkish environment minister Murat Kurum, acknowledged at the closing session that «there is no agreement on this text», because some countries were opposed.

What went wrong? The usual thing, this time with no patch-up: fossil fuels. The last draft no longer spoke of moving away from coal, oil and gas — it only made an indirect reference to them, according to AFP. The European Union, Ireland, Panama, Vanuatu and the Marshall Islands refused to approve a text like that. On the other side, oil-producing countries, led by Saudi Arabia, blocked any mention of phasing out fossil fuels. Along the way, a global target for electrifying the economy and a $120 billion climate finance goal requested by developing countries also fell.

The reactions were harsh. UN Secretary-General António Guterres said he shared the «very deep frustration» of those who expected action from governments and warned that the climate crisis «will not wait» for consensus. Australia's Chris Bowen, who led the negotiations, spoke of «impossible-to-reconcile red lines» and a «very disappointing» outcome. The UN climate chief, Simon Stiell, admitted the summit «fell short», though he said there were decisions on issues such as the just transition — something the sources consulted do not detail.

What now? The discussion on how and when to move away from fossil fuels passes to the next conference, COP32, in Ethiopia. Meanwhile, around 60 countries had already begun working on this outside the COPs, at a conference in April, according to AFP. In other words: the way forward may increasingly lie with groups of countries willing to move ahead, rather than waiting for unanimity.

Prompts sent to AI models were copied for months: OpenRouter drops three providers after a trap set by researchers

Fact confirmed by OpenRouter and by three providers · providers dropped not identified · full extent yet to be established · 22/11/2026

For months, someone was reading what companies and developers write to AI models. OpenRouter, a platform that routes requests to dozens of companies hosting AI models, announced on Sunday 22 November that it had dropped three of those providers and removed 125 services from its «zero data retention» option, the guarantee that prompts are not stored. The decision comes after researchers at the cybersecurity company Dragos detected prompt theft at at least three providers, which did not know they had been attacked.

Continue readingShow less

How was it discovered? Between July and November, Dragos sent around two million prompts with hidden «bait»: unique links and fake credentials. If anyone used one of those secrets, it proved they had read the prompt. According to The Hacker News, the earliest intrusion began in mid-July. In the most serious case, some two hundred access keys to Amazon's cloud (AWS) were stolen at the end of October and tested within a few hours by the attacker, who tried to reach data and create new users.

Three providers have already explained what happened to them. Phala says an attacker controlled one of its servers and that prompts to a DeepSeek model between 21 October and 8 November should be treated as exposed. Together AI says an AI coding agent mistakenly published a credential on GitHub, which gave access to samples of prompts from June and July. Novita speaks of a configuration error that sent prompts to another company. OpenRouter insists its own systems were not breached, but does not say which three providers it dropped.

Why does this matter? Many people paste code, logs and passwords into these prompts. The advice given to affected customers is to change all the keys, passwords and credentials they have written and check for unusual access. «A privacy option doesn't help when the attacker is already inside», summed up Dragos's head of research, HD Moore, quoted by The Hacker News.

AI agents used by China-linked hackers found a flaw in medical imaging software on their own and took over a university server, says Amazon

Amazon's account, not independently confirmed · attribution to China with moderate confidence · fix not yet available as an installable release · 20/11/2026

A group of hackers used AI agents to find and exploit a previously unknown flaw in medical imaging software, and gained full control of a university server. The account comes from Amazon's threat intelligence team, released on 20 November. The company says it suspects, with moderate confidence, attackers linked to China: the instructions were written in simplified Chinese and the clocks were set to Beijing time.

Continue readingShow less

The target was MONAI Label, an open-source program used to annotate medical scans and train AI models. According to Amazon, the tool used, called Cairn, puts several AI agents to work like a penetration-testing team: the operator chooses the target and the goal, and the agents decide how to get there. In about 11 minutes they reached the flaw, which allowed files to be written outside the intended folder without asking for a password. In another session, they used it to gain administrator access. A second university was reportedly attacked in the same way, but this is not confirmed.

How did it come to light? The attacker's control panel was left open on the internet without a password, with instructions and history in plain view. «The agents planned, executed and adjusted their approach as they learned», Amazon's security chief, CJ Moses, told The Record. Amazon did not name the universities, which are in East Asia, and says it has no evidence that patient data was taken.

What's missing: the flaw (CVE-2026-133968, severity 9.8 out of 10) was fixed in the code on 19 October but, according to The Hacker News, there is still no new release ready to install. Until then, the advice is not to leave these servers exposed to the internet and to switch on authentication, which is off by default. It is one more case in a recent series of attacks using AI tools, after the FBI's warning about the ARTEX tool.

Spies suspected of links to China used an unknown flaw in VMware vCenter for two months; deadline for US agencies to patch ends today

Attacks confirmed by Google/Mandiant · fix available · CISA deadline for federal agencies ends on 23 Nov · 20/11/2026

An espionage group suspected of links to China exploited a previously unknown flaw in VMware vCenter, the software many companies use to manage their virtual machines, for more than two months. The discovery comes from Mandiant, Google's incident response team, which published the details on 19 November, the day Broadcom, VMware's owner, released the fix. The US cybersecurity agency (CISA) added the flaw to its list of vulnerabilities already being exploited and gave federal agencies until today, Monday 23 November, to fix it.

Continue readingShow less

How did the attack work? According to Google, the group, which the company calls ARMORY CASTLE, first got in through another flaw, in a BeyondTrust remote support program. It then moved on to vCenter from early September, using its own tool. There it stole the key used to sign logins, created an administrator account and managed to get around two-step authentication. Finally, it reached the physical servers and took copies of Windows account databases.

The flaw (CVE-2026-123890) has a severity of 9.8 out of 10. It is fixed in versions 8.0 and 9 of vCenter, but version 7, which lost support in October 2025, is not getting a public fix despite being vulnerable, according to SecurityWeek. Google has not identified the victims or the sector.

Added to the same CISA list, with the same deadline, were serious flaws in Cisco Expressway, used for video conferencing, and in Apache Roller, a blogging program, both already used in attacks. In other words: anyone running these systems should update now, and not just in the US.

OpenAI's chief scientist proposes industry-wide limits to hold back more powerful AI models until there are ways to monitor them

Proposal published by OpenAI on Sunday · backed by Sam Altman · no numerical limits and no other companies signed up · 22/11/2026

AI companies should agree on limits that none of them crosses until they know how to monitor what their models are doing. The proposal comes from Jakub Pachocki, OpenAI's chief scientist, in a piece published on Sunday 22 November on the company's website, according to Reuters and Bloomberg. Chief executive Sam Altman backed it on X and called it «the most important thing» OpenAI has published in a long time.

Continue readingShow less

How would these limits work? Pachocki suggests using «perplexity», a measure of how well a model guesses the next word in a text (the lower it is, the more capable the model), combined with other measurements. Companies would commit not to pass a first threshold until the ability to understand and monitor AI behaviour reached a concrete target, defined in a common standard.

Why does this matter? Pachocki warns that AI itself could bring fundamental advances in training methods as early as 2027, faster than safety tools are progressing. In September, in an earlier essay, he had already argued for «extreme caution» and voluntary slowdowns when safety has not been demonstrated.

What we still don't know. According to the reports available, the proposal does not set values for the limits or the targets, and does not say who would check compliance. Nor has there been, so far, any public reaction from other companies, such as Anthropic or Google. For now, it is a proposal — not a commitment made by the industry.

The story was also reported by Bloomberg.

Continue readingShow less

What are they proposing? That Washington deals with national and international risks and that states keep room to protect their residents. For now, it is a request. There is no bill up for a vote, and the sources consulted do not indicate how Congress or the White House will respond.

José Clemente Orozco, the muralist who painted Mexico with one hand: born 143 years ago, he left a «Man of Fire» on the ceiling of a chapel

Born and died
23 November 1883, in Zapotlán el Grande (now Ciudad Guzmán), in the state of Jalisco, Mexico; 7 September 1949, in Mexico City
Field
Mural painting (fresco), caricature, drawing and printmaking
Essential work
Murals at the National Preparatory School, Mexico City (1923–27); «Prometheus», Pomona College, California (1930); «The Epic of American Civilization», Dartmouth College, USA (1932–34); «Catharsis», Palace of Fine Arts, Mexico City (1934); frescoes at the Hospicio Cabañas, Guadalajara, with «Man of Fire» (1938–39)
Now
Today, 23 November, marks 143 years since his birth

143 years ago today, on 23 November 1883, José Clemente Orozco was born in Mexico. He was one of the three great names of Mexican muralism, alongside Diego Rivera and David Alfaro Siqueiros. The Encyclopaedia Britannica considers him the most important 20th-century muralist to paint in fresco, that is, directly onto a wall while the plaster is still wet. And he did it with one hand: he lost his left hand while still young, in an accident.

Continue readingShow less

Orozco was born in Zapotlán el Grande, in the state of Jalisco, and moved with his family to Mexico City as a child. He was fascinated by the satirical prints of José Guadalupe Posada and took evening drawing classes at the Academy of San Carlos, but his father pushed him towards agronomy and architectural drawing. Everything changed with an explosion: according to PBS, while preparing fireworks for Independence Day, he injured his hand and an eye, and his left hand ended up being amputated. He went back to the academy to study painting and earned a living as a caricaturist for opposition newspapers during the Mexican Revolution. Between 1917 and 1919 he was in the USA, where he even painted cinema posters; before that, according to PBS, customs officials at the Texas border destroyed about two-thirds of the works he was carrying.

His style is dramatic and monumental: twisted bodies, strong colours, flames, machines, crowds. Orozco began with Mexican themes but gradually moved on to universal subjects — war, oppression, progress that crushes people — with an often bleak outlook. Britannica notes the influence of Michelangelo and Byzantine mosaics. In the 1930s he painted in the USA: «Prometheus» at Pomona College, in California (1930), described by PBS as the first true fresco painted in the country, and «The Epic of American Civilization», 24 panels covering the four walls of a room in the Dartmouth College library, designated a US National Historic Landmark in 2013.

His most famous work is in Guadalajara. Between 1938 and 1939 he painted 57 frescoes in the chapel of the Hospicio Cabañas, a former orphanage from the early 19th century. At the centre of the dome is «Man of Fire», a figure wrapped in flames that seems to rise towards the sky. UNESCO, which put the building on the World Heritage List, calls these frescoes a masterpiece of Mexican art. One more curiosity: Orozco died of heart failure in 1949, aged 65, while still working on a mural for a social housing complex, according to PBS.

03 / Did you know…?

Did you know that the premiere of «The Rite of Spring», in Paris, in 1913, ended in shouting, whistling and shoving — and that today it is one of the most performed works of the 20th century?

Did you know that one of the most famous premieres in the history of music and dance was a scandal? It happened in Paris, on 29 May 1913, at the Théâtre des Champs-Élysées. The Ballets Russes, the company of the Russian impresario Sergei Diaghilev, were presenting «The Rite of Spring», a ballet with music by Igor Stravinsky and choreography by Vaslav Nijinsky. The sets and costumes were by the painter Nicholas Roerich. As soon as the music started, the audience began to laugh, protest and whistle.

Continue readingShow less

What was so shocking? To begin with, the music. It opens with a bassoon playing a very high note, so high that it sounds strange. Then come chords that clash with one another and rhythms with accents where no one expects them. Then, the dance. Instead of light ballerinas on pointe, Nijinsky had the dancers stamping their feet, with heavy jumps, knees turned inwards and clenched fists. The story was not a peaceful one either: in an ancient, pagan Russia, a tribe chooses a young girl, who dances until she dies, as a sacrifice to the gods of spring.

The uproar was so great that the dancers could no longer hear the orchestra. The poet Jean Cocteau, who was in the audience, recalled that people laughed, booed and made animal noises. According to History Today, Diaghilev switched the house lights on and off to try to calm people down, and Nijinsky stood on a chair in the wings shouting out the counts to the dancers. Stravinsky, furious, left the stalls and watched the rest from the wings. There are reports that the police were called, but the number of arrests that is sometimes quoted is not confirmed by all sources — and some historians think part of the commotion came from the rivalry between the elegant audience and the bohemian audience, who were there on opposite sides.

And afterwards? The original choreography was danced only seven times before being replaced, and it disappeared from the stage for decades, until the Joffrey Ballet, in the USA, reconstructed it in 1987. The music had better luck: a year later, in 1914, played in concert in Paris, it was a triumph and Stravinsky is said to have been carried on the shoulders of his admirers. Today, «The Rite of Spring» is seen as one of the starting points of modern music and contemporary dance. In other words: sometimes, booing is just the sound of someone arriving too early.

04 / Culture on the move

Bons Sons: for 20 years, a village of 800 people near Tomar has turned itself into a festival of Portuguese music run by the neighbours themselves

Origins
First edition in 2006, organised by the Sport Clube Operário de Cem Soldos (SCOCS), the village association
Where
Cem Soldos, municipality of Tomar (Santarém district); ten stages spread across streets, squares, threshing floors and back gardens
Field
Portuguese music (Portuguese artists only), local development and community life
Participants
Village residents, SCOCS members (more than a thousand) and volunteers; 600 to 700 people involved in the 2026 edition, which expected around 32,000 visitors
Now
The 13th edition, marking 20 years, ran from 6 to 9 August 2026; since the end of 2024 the festival has been biennial

For four days, every two years, Cem Soldos changes its face and opens up to everyone. This village of around 800 people, near Tomar, has organised Bons Sons since 2006, a festival of Portuguese music only, spread over ten stages among the houses. It is not run by a concert promoter: it is run by the local association, the Sport Clube Operário de Cem Soldos, with the neighbours as hosts. In August 2026 it turned 20.

Continue readingShow less

The motto says it all: «Vem viver a aldeia» (Come and live the village). The stages are in streets, squares, threshing floors, vegetable gardens and an auditorium. According to a 2017 report by Observador, an old threshing floor belonging to a village family became one of the main stages, first used by Capitão Fausto in 2012, and the old grain store became an exhibition room. There are back gardens that turn into restaurants, scouts who set up spaces for babies and grandmothers who sew the souvenirs featuring the festival mascot, a lizard. At this year's edition, the 13th, the village welcomed names such as Rita Redshoes, Quinta do Bill, Luca Argel, Cacique'97 and a project that brought together Amélia Muge, Júlio Pereira, Rão Kyao and other musicians. The four-day pass, with camping, cost 70 euros.

The idea goes beyond the concerts. In 2017, according to Observador, money from the festival was already paying for children's activities and expenses at the village health post; and, according to the Lusa news agency, Bons Sons helps keep the population, the primary school and the nursery school going. It was biennial until 2014, became annual and, at the end of 2024, went back to being held every two years. The organisers explained the decision by the wish to give the volunteer teams more time and to create other projects in the village. The then director, Miguel Atalaia, said that investing in the festival's longevity «is believing in national artistic creation».

Why does it matter? In a country where many villages are losing people, Bons Sons shows another way of running a festival: small, community-made and with Portuguese music only, from well-known names to bands at the start of their careers. At 20, the leadership has passed to a younger generation. Sérgio Alves, who shares the artistic direction with João Rufino, told RTP that the festival is, in itself, «a resistance movement». And Filipe Cartaxo, president of the association, issued a warning: if the village is lost, «that is no longer Bons Sons».
