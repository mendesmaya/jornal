Spain goes to the polls tomorrow: polls give victory to Feijóo's PP and Vox nearly doubling, but Sánchez says he can still win

Election set for Sunday 29 Nov (fact) · PP victory and a PP–Vox majority are poll forecasts · 27/11/2026

Spaniards choose a new parliament tomorrow, Sunday 29 November, in a snap general election. The campaign ended yesterday, Friday: the Socialist prime minister, Pedro Sánchez, closed it at a rally in Madrid with around 12,000 people, and the leader of the People's Party (PP, centre-right), Alberto Núñez Feijóo, in Seville, according to RTP. The polls give victory to the PP, but without an absolute majority, which requires 176 of the 350 seats.

What do the polls say? The average cited by Observador, a week before the vote, put the PP at around 31% (about 129 seats), Sánchez's PSOE at around 28% and the far-right Vox at around 19%, which would almost double its current 33 seats. Together, PP and Vox would exceed 190 seats. The gap between PP and PSOE has narrowed from around six to just over three points since the election was called, and two polls even allowed for a Socialist victory. The left-wing platform Frente Amplia, led by the former mayor of Barcelona Ada Colau, was below 7%. These are forecasts, not results.

Sánchez called this election on 5 October, two days after parliament voted down two decrees with housing measures, according to RTP. The parliamentary term would only have ended in July 2027. Housing is Spaniards' main concern, ahead of immigration and the economy, according to a CIS survey (the public centre for sociological research) cited by Euronews; rents have risen by an average of around 44% in five years, according to the Idealista property portal.

In the final hours of the campaign, each rallied their own voters. «This election is about the need for Spain to move forward and not go back to the past», said Sánchez, who demanded that the PP accept the results. Feijóo promised «a new era in Spain» if voters vote together, and the Vox leader, Santiago Abascal, warned that «ballot boxes are not filled with favourable polls», according to RTP. Feijóo has already admitted he could govern with Vox, with «red lines», and the two parties have governed together in four regions since this year. If no candidate wins the confidence of parliament within two months of the first vote, there will be new elections.

SAP confirms cyberattack: hackers allegedly used employees' keys to get into the German software giant's code

Incident confirmed by SAP · access to source code reported by the German press, not confirmed by the company · 27/11/2026

SAP, Europe's largest software company, based in Walldorf, Germany, has confirmed that it was the target of a cyberattack. The magazine Der Spiegel revealed the case on Thursday 26 November: according to its sources, the hackers stole employees' «tokens» — digital keys that prove who is logging into a system — and used them to copy and alter company source code stored on GitHub, the platform where programmers work on code.

Continue readingShow less

What SAP says. The company confirmed the incident but did not answer detailed questions. It says it detected «suspicious activity», acted immediately to protect customers and has so far found no signs of unauthorised access to customers' business data. It did not say who is behind the attack, how they got in, or whether the problem has been resolved.

Internally, the case has a code name: «Project Granite». According to Handelsblatt, SAP restricted access to GitHub, halted automated development processes and replaced the digital keys used to certify that its software is authentic. The German news agency dpa saw part of the internal communications and says employees were warned days before the case became public. The German press mentions the possibility that the attackers used AI «agents», but for now that is only a suspicion.

Why does this matter? SAP software runs the accounts, payroll and supply chains of thousands of large companies and public bodies around the world, including in Portugal. It remains to be seen whether the code was actually accessed and whether any changes reached customers. The case comes in the same week that Bloomberg reported SAP is considering cutting at least 10,000 jobs in an AI-focused restructuring.

Another case in US government systems: hackers got into Cloud One, the US Air Force's main «cloud» computing platform. Continue readingShow less

What is at stake. Cloud One has existed since 2017 and runs on servers from Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Google. It hosts applications for military operations, personnel, logistics and maintenance — including, according to Reuters, a system that receives data from air defence radars and another that controls munitions. One of the sources says the attackers exploited a software flaw to reach the platform's «control panel».

What we don't yet know: who the hackers are, how far they got and whether they took data. Investigators have asked US Cyber Command to assess the damage. The Air Force said only that it does not comment on the security or status of specific networks, and that it takes measures to protect information. It is at least the seventh recent computer incident at US government bodies, after cases at FinCEN, the Treasury agency that receives money-laundering alerts, and at the CIA.

Japanese newspaper Nikkei confirms leak of 5.4 million readers' data through a marketing tool

Leak confirmed by Nikkei · no known misuse · cards and passwords not included · 27/11/2026

Nikkei, Japan's largest business newspaper, confirmed on Friday 27 November that personal data of 5,399,986 registered users had leaked. The problem was not in the newspaper's systems but in KARTE, a marketing tool from the Japanese company Plaid, to which Nikkei handed this data to analyse readers' behaviour.

Continue readingShow less

What data leaked? Gender, year and month of birth, and email addresses and phone numbers — the latter two «converted» into other strings of characters. Nikkei says the originals did not leak and that the conversion cannot be undone, but did not explain how it was done. Credit card numbers and passwords were not among the data handed to Plaid. So far, the newspaper says it has no sign that the information has been misused.

The timeline: Plaid detected third-party access on 13 November and shut down the whole KARTE service; two days later it notified Nikkei, which made the suspicion public on the 17th. According to Plaid, the attackers exploited a vulnerability in an internal process. Other Japanese companies that use the same tool, such as Sumitomo Mitsui bank and the airline Peach, were also affected.

In other words: an attack on a single supplier reached millions of people who had never heard of it. Nikkei will contact those affected directly and report the case to Japan's data protection authority. Plaid advises people to be wary of suspicious emails and text messages.

Continue readingShow less

What is distillation? It means training a model on the answers of another, more advanced one — a cheap way of catching up with the competition. Anthropic said in September it had detected almost 200 million conversations with Claude linked to suspected distillation, which it attributed to Alibaba, Moonshot and Z.AI. OpenAI has made similar accusations. Moonshot denies that the results of its Kimi K3 model come from this.

The delays, according to Reuters' sources: Z.AI's next big model, GLM-5.5, was planned for November and may slip to 2027. At Moonshot, the usual six-month gap between models could stretch to 12 or more if it loses access to computers abroad; Kimi K4 may slip to mid-2027.

The backdrop is a US Department of Commerce rule, in force since 19 November, that requires a licence for data centres abroad with more than around 750 chips equivalent to Nvidia's H100. It was because of this rule that Nvidia halted server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries, as we reported yesterday.

Programme launched · first phase at IEFP until 31 December · 600,000 target is a goal, not a result · 26/11/2026

Civil servants are going to learn to use artificial intelligence. The target is to reach 600,000 people by 2030, according to a member of the board of the INA, the National Institute of Administration, quoted by Lusa.

Continue readingShow less

How does it work? Each person starts with an assessment that directs them to the right level. The training is mostly online and self-paced, with short videos, demonstrations and exercises linked to work in public services. Topics range from data protection and ethics to the use of generative AI and task automation. The introductory course and the intermediate path are free.

The first phase is for staff at the IEFP, the employment institute, and is due to be completed by 31 December. From 2027, the training will gradually extend to other bodies. The programme is coordinated by the INA and ARTE, the agency for the state's technological reform, with input from Microsoft, Google, Amazon Web Services and Instituto Superior Técnico.

The aim, the Government says, is for services to spend less time on repetitive tasks and serve citizens and businesses better. «State Reform is done with people», said the Assistant Minister for State Reform, Gonçalo Saraiva Matias.

How was it discovered? One of the companies, Shanghai Shuoyao, put up one of these server racks as collateral for a loan of 96 million yuan (about $14 million). Shuoyao says it is merely a service provider to Megaspeed; Gigabyte will not say to whom it sold the rack; Nvidia did not respond.

Why does this matter? It shows how hard it is to control where AI chips go once they are sold — precisely what the new US rules are trying to stop. Nvidia's chief executive, Jensen Huang, is due to testify publicly before Congress on 3 December.

Why would anyone paint with mummies? For the colour. It was a warm brown, leaning towards reddish, somewhere between raw and burnt umber, and it mixed well with other paints. Depending on the recipe, it could have redder, yellower or even purplish tones. It had one flaw: it faded easily. The mummies were dug up and smuggled out of Egypt, and there are accounts that some of the paint may have been made from bodies that were not Egyptian at all.

Many painters did not even know what was on their brush. When Edward Burne-Jones discovered where the brown came from, he was so disturbed that, according to his wife, he insisted on burying the tube in the garden — and planted a daisy on top. Eugène Delacroix is said to have used it too, for example in «Liberty Leading the People», but that is hard to confirm: testing the paint in a picture without damaging it is rarely possible, so most of these attributions remain unproven.

The paint lasted longer than you might think. The London firm Roberson & Co. kept it in its catalogue until 1933 and made its last tube in 1964. That year, a director of the company explained that there was no way to carry on: they could not get any more mummies. «Mummy brown» is still sold today, but it is made from minerals. Next time you see a beautiful brown in a 19th-century painting, you will know what question to ask.

Porta-Jazz: 16 years ago, Porto's jazz musicians came together in an association — and today they have their own venue, a record label and a festival

In Porto, jazz musicians decided 16 years ago not to wait for someone to open the door for them. In 2010 they founded the Associação Porta-Jazz, so they could play original music regularly and reach new audiences. Today the association has its own venue on the Praça da República, a record label and an annual festival, which in February this year reached its 16th edition, at the Teatro Rivoli.

Continue readingShow less

The basic idea is simple: a collective of musicians who organise themselves to create, play and show what they do. The first public step was the Festival Porta-Jazz itself, back in 2010. In 2012 the label was born, Carimbo Porta-Jazz, which releases records by musicians in the circle and which, according to the Teatro Municipal do Porto, has now passed a hundred releases. In 2013 the Sala Porta-Jazz opened, for rehearsals, residencies, concerts and workshops.

The association gets around the city. There is a jam session (an open improvisation session) on Fridays and a weekly series on Tuesdays at Maus Hábitos, «Jazz à Mesa» (Jazz at the Table), with meetings between musicians and new repertoire. There have also been summer concerts in the gardens of the Palácio de Cristal, a free jazz course with the Conservatório de Música do Porto and a partnership with Guimarães Jazz. Since 2023, the regular programme has been held at the Espaço Porta-Jazz, at no. 156 Praça da República.

Why does this matter? Because jazz is music that lives on being played live and on people meeting, and a city needs small stages where that happens every week, not just at festivals. Porta-Jazz also connects Porto to other scenes: at the last festival it hosted partner collectives from abroad, such as the Swiss AMR, from Geneva, and created its own group projects, such as Ursa Maior. For anyone who wants to take a look, the listings are on the association's website and on Agenda Porto.
