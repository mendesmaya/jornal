Story of the week · 01 / World · Thu 19 Nov

Shenzhen: Xi, Trump and Putin meet for the first time and leave with a «peace declaration» that leaves out Ukraine

Fact: summit and joint declaration · text has no deadlines or mechanisms · no new meeting announced · 19/11/2026

It was the picture of the week. On Thursday 19 November, Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin met in Shenzhen, in southern China, on the sidelines of the APEC summit (the Asia-Pacific economic cooperation forum). It was the first three-way summit between the leaders of China, the US and Russia. The meeting lasted about an hour and a half and ended with a «trilateral peace declaration» covering UN reform, more oil production and dialogue on nuclear risks — but it does not mention the war in Ukraine or the one in Iran.

So what does the text actually say? It has four points, with no timetable, no mechanism to make anyone comply and no arms-reduction commitments, according to the South China Morning Post. The three countries defend the immunity of leaders and say international courts, such as the International Criminal Court, «must not abuse their jurisdiction». Putin himself admitted the declaration sets no ambitious goals and that no one will be forced to follow it. Xi spoke of a «fruitful» meeting; Trump rated it «12 out of 10».

In Europe and in Kyiv, the reading was different. French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot said the three leaders «disagree on everything». Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky responded to Trump calling Putin a «friend»: «For us, he is an enemy.» Hours after leaving Shenzhen, Russia launched one of the week's biggest attacks on Ukraine (see next story).

What has changed since: On Saturday 21 November, Trump posted photos from the summit on Truth Social, including a handshake with Putin captioned «My Friend», drawing criticism from senators of both parties, according to DPA. No new tariffs were announced at the bilateral meeting with Xi; the two countries agreed to draw up an action plan for «strategic stability», according to China Briefing. No new three-way meeting has been scheduled.

02 / World · Fri 20 Nov

War in Ukraine escalates: Crimea bridge cut, Kyiv bridges hit and Russian missiles fall in Poland

Facts (attacks, impact in Poland) · Russian intent disputed · Poland has not requested Article 4 consultations · 20/11/2026

The week began with a Ukrainian strike and ended with NATO on alert. On the night of 16 to 17 November, Ukraine attacked the bridge linking Russia to occupied Crimea with drones and missiles and cut the road link; Moscow admitted «damage». Russia responded with strikes on Kyiv that hit bridges over the Dnipro on 17 November and again on 20 November. The 20 November attack — more than 400 drones and more than 110 missiles — killed at least eight people, according to Ukrainian authorities quoted by AP.

Continue readingShow less

In the same early hours, two Russian missiles and a jet-powered drone came down in three villages in south-eastern Poland, less than 50 kilometres from the border. No one was hurt. One of the missiles, a Kh-101, was recovered in Nowy Lipowiec. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk speaks of an «intentional provocation»; Trump said Putin dropped them «by mistake». The Kremlin has not commented.

On Saturday 21, NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte went to Warsaw and promised the Alliance will defend «every inch» of Polish territory. NATO's top military commander in Europe will assess the gaps and adjust defences if needed. Poland has not invoked Article 4 of the treaty (which triggers formal consultations among allies), according to Al Jazeera; the case will be discussed by the North Atlantic Council on Monday.

What has changed since: On the negotiations front, the Wall Street Journal reported on 20 November, citing anonymous sources, that US envoys discussed offering Ukraine NATO membership in exchange for the Donbas, or freezing the front line with security guarantees; Moscow rejected both. There is no date for a new round. The violence continued over the weekend: on Sunday 22, a Russian drone destroyed several floors of an apartment block in Dymer, near Kyiv, and people may be trapped under the rubble, according to the Kyiv Post and Ukrinform. Russia says rail traffic on the Crimea bridge has been restored; there is no date for the road.

03 / Portugal · Sat 21 Nov

2027 budget: Socialists and Chega force through pensions, zero VAT and frozen tuition fees, and the Government warns the deficit is back

Facts (detailed votes) · deficit estimates are the Government's · final vote on 24 Nov at 3pm · 21/11/2026

Budget week went badly for Portugal's Government. From 18 November, in the stage where amendments are voted one by one, the Socialist Party (PS) and the far-right Chega teamed up several times against the governing PSD and CDS-PP: they passed permanent pension rises, a public guarantee for rents, measures to restore civil servants' years of service, zero VAT on a basket of foods from January to March 2027 and a freeze on tuition fees at public universities.

Continue readingShow less

What does it cost? Finance Minister Joaquim Miranda Sarmento says «with the PS measures, the deficit returns»: he estimates between 0.3% and 0.7% of GDP in 2027, against a small surplus in the Government's proposal. On Saturday he conceded the «most likely scenario» is that the Government manages, «with a lot of effort», to keep the accounts balanced. Prime Minister Luís Montenegro accused PS and Chega of «irresponsibility»; PS leader José Luís Carneiro insists the party's proposals are «sustainable».

What has changed since: On 21 November, the final overall vote was moved from 10am to 3pm on Tuesday 24, because of Chega's party convention this weekend; on Monday the plenary starts at 9am. The PS has said it will abstain, which should ensure the budget passes; at the convention, André Ventura refused to «guarantee» the budget and called the PS the Government's «crutch». On Friday, Moody's upgraded Portugal's credit rating to A2.

04 / World · Sun 22 Nov

COP31 in overtime: final text of the climate summit goes to a vote without mentioning fossil fuels

Final text presented · approval scheduled for the morning of 22 Nov · outcome not confirmed at time of publication · 22/11/2026

The UN climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, was due to end on Friday 20 November and went into a second day of overtime. On Saturday night, COP31 President Murat Kurum presented a text that, he said, «all parties» can accept; the approval session was scheduled for Sunday morning, 22 November. According to AP, this possibly final version does not mention fossil fuels, the main cause of global warming.

Continue readingShow less

What changed during the week? The first draft spoke of moving away from fossil fuels; the 19 November draft removed those references. Instead, it proposes that electricity should make up 35% of the energy used worldwide by 2035, gives prominence to carbon capture and acknowledges for the first time that the 1.5°C limit will be overshot. The European Union threatened to block a text that forgets oil, coal and gas, and several countries, such as Panama and Vanuatu, said they would not accept it as it stood.

What has changed since: Kurum insists concerns about fossil fuels and adaptation finance were «addressed», but the final text was not public when Al Jazeera reported. Portugal played a role in the talks: Environment Minister Maria da Graça Carvalho negotiated the just-transition file for the EU and announced a further €3.2 million for climate funds.

05 / Technology · Fri 20 Nov

Nvidia: after Huang's exit, record revenue and, in the end, its worst week on the stock market since 2008

Facts (results, share fall) · SEC investigation reported with anonymous sources · SEC and Nvidia declined to comment · 20/11/2026

Nvidia, the biggest maker of artificial intelligence (AI) chips, had a week of extremes. On Tuesday 17 November, it presented its first results without Jensen Huang at the helm: revenue of $115.8 billion in three months, more than double a year earlier. The board ruled out the founder's return, despite a letter with thousands of signatures calling for it. On Friday 20, the shares fell 14% to $125.53 and ended the week down 23% — the worst week since October 2008.

Continue readingShow less

Why did they fall so much? The slide accelerated after Reuters reported, citing two sources, that the SEC, the US securities regulator, is examining possible accounting problems at the company. The SEC did not comment and Nvidia did not respond to journalists' questions. The company had already mentioned in its results statement an internal investigation by an independent committee of the board.

What has changed since: California's attorney general subpoenaed Nvidia on 19 November for information on chips that allegedly reached China and on whether it misled investors, and a law firm announced a shareholder class action. Board chairman Harvey Jones says the search for a permanent chief executive is under way; Colette Kress remains interim leader and no names are known.

07 / Cybersecurity · Thu 19 Nov

AI in the service of cyberattacks: from Google to the European Commission, the week the warning became reality

Facts (reports, confirmed incident) · data theft at the Commission not confirmed · perpetrators unknown · 19/11/2026

This was the week artificial intelligence showed up, in black and white, on the attackers' side. On 16 November, Google said it had seen the first cyberattack carried out from start to finish by an AI system, with no human involvement: it found flaws, prepared the attack and executed it against French websites. On the 17th, OpenAI launched its GPT-6.2 models and admitted they reach the «critical» cybersecurity risk level, the highest on its scale, and therefore come with stronger safeguards.

Continue readingShow less

Then came Brussels. On 19 November, the European Commission confirmed that the systems its staff use for pay, leave and working hours had been attacked with a publicly available AI tool. The attack was detected on 22 October. According to spokesperson Thomas Regnier, there is so far no evidence of a data breach affecting staff as a whole, and no known ransom demand. In Germany, the cybersecurity agency (BSI) raised its alert level and warned of a serious flaw in Java, «Java4Shell», which requires thousands of companies to update their systems.

What has changed since: As of Sunday 22, the Commission had not confirmed whether data was stolen or who was behind the attack. Politico reported that the EU's response team, CERT-EU, detected the ARTEX tool — the same one the FBI had warned about — but the Commission has not officially confirmed this.

08 / Portugal · Sat 21 Nov

A week of rain and rough seas ends with five districts on red warning and more than a thousand incidents, with no casualties

Facts (IPMA warnings) · Civil Protection tally cited by a single source · dry weather forecast until Monday · 21/11/2026

It rained almost all week on mainland Portugal, with waves of up to 10 metres on the northern and central coast from Wednesday and a flash flood in Manteigas, in the Serra da Estrela, that left people homeless on 19 November. The worst came on Saturday 21: IPMA, Portugal's weather service, put Coimbra, Guarda and Viseu on red rain warning — the highest level — and later added Leiria and Santarém, making five districts in all until 6pm.

Continue readingShow less

By 6pm on Saturday, Civil Protection had recorded more than a thousand incidents, mostly flooding, more than 700 of them in the Lisbon and Tagus Valley region, according to Observador. There were no casualties. In Lisbon, the Segunda Circular ring road flooded again and some tunnels were closed; the Portugal–Uruguay rugby match at the Restelo was cancelled because the pitch was waterlogged. In Manteigas, eight streets near the stream remained closed as a precaution.

What has changed since: Late on Saturday afternoon, IPMA lifted the red warnings. Sunday and Monday should be dry and colder, with minimum temperatures falling 2 to 5 degrees; rain may return on Tuesday 24 with a new cold front, according to IPMA as cited by Observador.

09 / Portugal · Sat 21 Nov

Public-sector strike, a CGTP march in the rain and more stoppages planned for budget vote day

Facts (strike, march) · turnout according to unions · no official attendance figures · 21/11/2026

On Friday 20 November, public administration workers held a national strike called by the Frente Comum union front. Rubbish went uncollected in several municipalities and many schools were closed; the Frente Comum spoke of «strong» turnout, without giving a national percentage. The unions want rises of at least 15%, with a minimum of €150, and call the Government's offer «cents».

Continue readingShow less

On Saturday 21, it was the turn of the CGTP, Portugal's largest trade union confederation. Several thousand people marched in the rain from Saldanha to Restauradores, in Lisbon, under the slogan «A new direction for Portugal», calling for higher wages, an end to precarious work, price controls and more investment in housing, health and education. No official figures were released. CGTP leader Tiago Oliveira warned that «workers will not forget» the parties that let the budget pass.

What has changed since: The protests continue this week. CP train drivers have a strike called from 23 to 27 November, with a full stoppage on the 24th and 25th. On the day of the final budget vote, Tuesday 24, there is a national strike called by unions in education (S.T.O.P.), nursing (SITEU) and retail (STGSSP) and by the MUDAR bank workers' movement, with protests outside Parliament, where the CGTP will also rally.

10 / Space · Sat 21 Nov

BepiColombo is now in orbit around Mercury, eight years after setting off

Fact: capture confirmed by the ESA team · science planned for April 2027 · 21/11/2026

BepiColombo has arrived. The mission of the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Japanese agency JAXA went into orbit around Mercury on Saturday 21 November, after firing its engines for 37 minutes to brake. The manoeuvre was scheduled for 18:24 Lisbon time. ESA's flight dynamics team announced «orbital capture» shortly afterwards. It is only the second mission ever to orbit the planet closest to the Sun, after NASA's MESSENGER.

Continue readingShow less

What next? The probe carries two spacecraft attached to each other: Europe's MPO and Japan's Mio. They will separate on 9 and 10 December and will only reach their final orbits in March 2027. Science should begin in April, if the timetable holds; ESA warns the dates may change. The mission carries Portuguese science and technology.
