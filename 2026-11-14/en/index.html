Europe picks team for Ukraine peace talks: UK, France, Germany, Italy and Poland, with the EU institutions

Decision announced by António Costa · Kremlin does not commit to a European presence; date of the round to be confirmed · 13/11/2026

European Union leaders decided on Friday 13 November, at an informal summit in Dublin, that Europe will be represented in the next round of negotiations to end the war in Ukraine by a team of five countries — the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Italy and Poland — accompanied by the European institutions. The announcement was made by the President of the European Council, António Costa, who called for Europe to speak “with one voice” and described the solution as “a team effort”. The leaders did not appoint a single envoy, although Ukraine and the US had asked for one.

According to Euronews, in addition to the five countries, the team will include the European Council, the European Commission and NATO, in a format already used in previous rounds between security advisers. The EU's High Representative for foreign policy, Kaja Kallas, claimed the role of envoy and said that “the Russians don't want me” because of her firm positions; the Commission President, Ursula von der Leyen, did not back her. The German chancellor, Friedrich Merz, asked Washington for “no surprises”, while the Italian prime minister, Giorgia Meloni, argued for a single envoy. The US envoy Jared Kushner said a single European representative would be “a bigger achievement”.

On the Russian side, the Kremlin spokesman, Dmitry Peskov, said Europe must first show it is willing to contribute to the peace process, and Moscow did not confirm the European participation announced the day before by the US envoys after their meeting with Vladimir Putin. RTÉ reports that the next round is expected to take place in Abu Dhabi next week, but the date has not been officially announced. On the same day, according to Ukraine's emergency services cited by Euronews, Russian drones hit a hospital in central Kyiv, forcing the evacuation of dozens of people, and an attack in Odesa killed two people and injured five.

01 / Cybersecurity 1/3

US freezes more than $1.2 billion stolen from Binance and dismantles North Korean fake video-call scheme that netted $400 million

Confirmed fact (Department of Justice and FBI announcement) · trail of the remaining funds still being followed; no arrests · 12/11/2026

The US Department of Justice (DOJ) and the FBI announced on Thursday 12 November two actions against cryptocurrency theft by hackers linked to North Korea. Under court order, more than $1.2 billion was seized or frozen out of the roughly $6.3 billion stolen in October from Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange — an attack the FBI attributes to the TraderTraitor group. On the same day, the FBI revealed it had dismantled the cloud infrastructure of a campaign it calls “FaceStealer”, which is said to have netted more than $400 million in 18 months.

Continue readingShow less

According to the DOJ, the $1.2 billion is part of $2.7 billion already frozen across several platforms, close to half of the Binance theft. The trail of the rest is still being followed, because the attackers keep moving the funds through cross-chain bridges, mixers and exchanges. The analytics firm Elliptic reported that they created their own worthless cryptocurrencies to push the money through decentralised exchanges, a technique already seen after the Bybit theft in 2025.

FaceStealer operated from stolen Telegram, LinkedIn, Slack or Discord accounts: the attackers sent meeting invitations to the victim's contacts, drew them into fake Zoom or Teams video calls, where they played recordings of earlier victims and fake videos (deepfakes) of well-known figures, and simulated an audio fault whose “fix” installed programs that stole cryptocurrency. Targets' cameras were secretly recorded to deceive the next victim; the FBI seized more than 900 of these recordings. The targets were mainly cryptocurrency companies and open-source software developers. The FBI says it does not know whether the scheme was used in the Binance attack and advises wariness of unexpected invitations, even from known contacts, and of requests to install something during a call.

02 / Cybersecurity 2/3

Hackers break into Check Point firewalls through PHP flaws and wipe servers of Israeli organisations

Attacks confirmed by Check Point · perpetrators yet to be established; total number of victims not disclosed · 13/11/2026

An as yet unidentified group exploited serious flaws to break into security gateways (firewalls) made by Check Point, one of the brands most used by companies and governments to protect their networks, and in some cases destroyed data, the company itself revealed on Friday 13 November. The attacks began on 30 October against Israeli organisations and spread on 2 November to companies in several countries and sectors. Check Point says it detected exploitation of one of the flaws at “a few dozen” customers.

Continue readingShow less

The attackers got in through the remote access portal (Mobile Access) via the “PHP2Shell” flaws (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619), two critical vulnerabilities in the PHP language that were patched on 21 October and that the US agency CISA already listed as exploited. They then used a previously unknown flaw in the Gaia system (CVE-2026-123039) to gain full control of the device, installed a discreet backdoor called TSGA, capable of deleting firewall logs, and, in the attacks in Israel, jumped to VMware ESXi servers, where they launched a program that wipes files. The attackers left a note with political messages that rejects any ransom negotiation.

Check Point links some of the tools to a set of programs that ESET tied to a China-linked group, but says it has no evidence to attribute the attacks with confidence and allows for the possibility of a “false flag” operation. The company urges immediate installation of the November update (Jumbo Hotfix) on all gateways and management servers, starting with those whose portal is exposed to the internet, and says any unexpected SSH connection from a gateway to internal servers should be treated as an intrusion.

03 / Cybersecurity 3/3

German parliament gives intelligence services powers to hack and sabotage foreign hackers' servers

Law passed by the Bundestag · critics raise the prospect of a constitutional challenge; remaining legislative steps not confirmed · 13/11/2026

The Bundestag, the German federal parliament, approved on Friday 13 November a reform of the intelligence services laws that allows the BND (the foreign intelligence service) not only to monitor threats abroad but to act against them — hacking foreign systems, sabotaging adversaries' supply chains and taking down servers of hacking groups backed by hostile states. The law passed with 309 votes in favour (308, according to The Record), 190 against and 71 abstentions.

Continue readingShow less

The BfV, the domestic intelligence service, gains more limited covert powers within the country, such as blocking or diverting data traffic, secretly entering homes under certain conditions and investigating organised crime networks that support extremism, but still cannot arrest people or use force. Both agencies will be able to analyse large volumes of data with artificial intelligence and compare faces with images published on the internet, and telecoms operators can be required to “freeze” traffic data. Hacking operations and active measures in Germany need prior authorisation from an independent oversight council, which grows from six to twelve members.

The interior minister, Alexander Dobrindt, called it the “reform of the century” and rejected accusations that a “secret police” is being created. The opposition says the law erases the separation between police and intelligence services, and critics point out that journalists and lawyers are left with weaker protections than doctors. The German press anticipates a possible battle in the Constitutional Court.

05 / Artificial Intelligence 2/3

OpenAI launches digital wallet so its AI agents can make purchases on users' behalf

OpenAI announcement · rollout limited to a small group of Pro subscribers in the US; each purchase requires approval · 13/11/2026

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on Friday 13 November, a payments wallet that lets its artificial intelligence agents — the “dots” — buy products online on users' behalf. The user saves a card once and approves each purchase with a security check, such as facial recognition or a fingerprint, after reviewing the seller, the items and the total. The feature was developed with American Express, Mastercard and Visa and, for now, is only available to a small group of Pro plan subscribers in the US, aged 18 or over, with a US card and billing address.

Continue readingShow less

According to OpenAI, the card networks replace the real card number with a payment code (a “token”), so neither the agent nor the shop sees the card details; card rewards and the right to dispute charges are retained. The wallet only works within the Dots agent tool. The company says it will start slowly in order to learn from real use and intends, later on, to allow pre-authorised purchases within spending limits, product categories and time periods set by the user.

Its reach depends on the shops: some, such as Amazon, block AI agents, and OpenAI has published guidance so that merchants do not bar them by mistake; prices, deliveries and returns remain the shops' responsibility. It is a fresh attempt by OpenAI at commerce, after it abandoned in March the Instant Checkout system, launched in September 2025, in a race that also includes Google, Meta and xAI.

06 / Artificial Intelligence 3/3

Claude helps decipher Kurt Gödel's notebooks and reveals hundreds of the logician's unknown theorems

Results announced by the researcher (The Guardian) · no scientific paper published and no independent review · 13/11/2026

The Finnish logician Jan von Plato, of the University of Helsinki, used Anthropic's artificial intelligence model Claude to analyse the 16 working notebooks that Kurt Gödel, one of the greatest logicians of the 20th century, wrote in the early 1940s — 1,009 pages of mathematics and logic, largely in a German shorthand that has fallen out of use. According to the account published by The Guardian on Friday 13 November, Claude identified 663 theorems that Gödel proved, sketched or merely imagined, many of them unknown until now.

Continue readingShow less

The project, funded by the European Union since 2017, had been advancing at about one page a week. In September, von Plato gave Claude his transcriptions and books and, within a few days, received an explanation of about 650 pages, which he says he reviewed page by page. Checked with the formal proof program Coq, about 400 theorems were confirmed as they stood, about 200 needed corrections or additional assumptions and about 50 were false. According to von Plato, the notebooks show that Gödel was “one step away” from proving the independence of the axiom of choice, a result that earned Paul Cohen the Fields Medal in 1966.

The figures are the researcher's own, and there has been no scientific publication yet nor any data allowing others to repeat the analysis. The logician Joel David Hamkins, of the University of Notre Dame, who spoke to the paper, noted that the similarity with later work shows that Gödel “was on the path”, not that he had the result, and that it will be for historians to divide the credit between the mathematician and the machine.

Communications failure grounds all flights in the US for more than four hours; government says new AI tool was not to blame

Confirmed fact (FAA) · technical cause under investigation; cyberattack considered unlikely but not entirely ruled out · 13/11/2026

The US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) ordered a halt on Friday 13 November to all take-offs at the country's airports, after a communications failure that affected several regional air traffic control centres. The stoppage lasted more than four hours and left thousands of passengers stranded: almost 5,000 flights were cancelled, according to ABC. It was the first nationwide stoppage since January 2023 and had knock-on effects in Mexico, which also temporarily restricted air operations.

Continue readingShow less

The transportation secretary, Sean Duffy, blamed old equipment: the system's switches and routers, still based on analogue telecommunications, became overloaded and began sending inconsistent data, which made the radars and communications unstable and led the FAA to shut the system down. Duffy asked Congress for $50 billion in emergency funding and said he expected normal service to be restored by Thanksgiving. The homeland security secretary, Markwayne Mullin, said there were no signs of an attack, but did not rule one out completely.

The failure came shortly after the FAA had expanded SMART, a new artificial intelligence tool that reorganises routes in real time, and Mullin at one point said the cybersecurity agency CISA was examining whether the system had contributed. The company that developed it, Air Space Intelligence, insisted that the stoppage “did not result” from SMART, and Duffy later stated that the tool played no role whatsoever.

Born
14 November 1907, Näs farm, near Vimmerby (Småland, Sweden); died in Stockholm on 28 January 2002, aged 94
Field

Key works
«Pippi Longstocking» (1945–1971), «The Children of Noisy Village» (Bullerby, 1946–1966), «Mio, My Son» (1954), «Karlsson-on-the-Roof» (1955–1972), Emil of Lönneberga (1963–1997), «The Brothers Lionheart» (1973), «Ronia, the Robber's Daughter» (1981)
Why now
Anniversary: 119 years since her birth, on 14 November 2026

Today, Saturday 14 November, marks 119 years since the birth of Astrid Lindgren, the Swedish writer who invented Pippi Longstocking, the red-haired girl who is the strongest in the world, lives alone with a horse and a monkey and obeys no one. Born Astrid Ericsson in 1907 on the Näs farm, on the outskirts of Vimmerby in southern Sweden, she wrote more than 40 books for children, translated into more than 90 languages, and is one of the most widely read authors in the world: sales estimates range from around 145 million to more than 180 million copies, depending on the source and the year.

Continue readingShow less

Her life began far from books. While still a teenager she worked at the local newspaper, the Vimmerby Tidning, where she published her first piece in 1921; at 18 she became pregnant, left her home town and went to Stockholm to train as a secretary, and her son, Lars, was born in Copenhagen and spent his first years with a foster family. In 1931 she married Sture Lindgren and worked as a secretary and for the Swedish intelligence services before turning to publishing. Pippi was born of a request: her daughter Karin, ill in bed, wanted to hear stories about a character whose name she herself had made up, and Astrid wrote them down as a present for the girl's tenth birthday, in 1944. Rejected by the publisher Bonniers, the manuscript won a competition run by Rabén & Sjögren in 1945, where the author would later be children's books editor from 1946 to 1970.

Lindgren's style rests on children who think for themselves, on anarchic humour and on absolute respect for the child's point of view, without moralising — and she does not shy away from difficult themes such as death, loneliness and fear, in books like «Mio, My Son» or «The Brothers Lionheart». Pippi even caused controversy among Swedish parents and teachers, who saw her as a bad example. Alongside the adventures of Emil, the children of Bullerby (Noisy Village), Karlsson-on-the-Roof and Ronia, her work gave rise to dozens of films and series and to a theme park in Vimmerby. She received the Hans Christian Andersen Award in 1958 and the Right Livelihood Award, the so-called alternative Nobel, in 1994.

Some curiosities show the writer's public weight. She wrote in shorthand, in the morning, in bed, before going to work. In 1976 she published a satirical tale, «Pomperipossa in Monismania», against a tax rule that obliged her to pay more than 100% tax on part of her income, and the case entered Swedish political debate; her campaign against corporal punishment is associated with the 1979 law, the first in the world to ban it, and her defence of farm animals inspired a 1988 law. In 1999 she was voted the most popular Swede, since 2015 her portrait has appeared on the 20-krona note and, after her death, the Swedish government created the Astrid Lindgren Memorial Award (ALMA), one of the most valuable prizes in the world for children's and young adult literature.

03 / Did you know…?

Did you know that the first animated feature film was not by Disney, but Argentine, from 1917 — and that no copy of it survives?

Many people believe that Disney's «Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs» (1937) was the first animated feature film in history. It was not: twenty years earlier, on 9 November 1917 — 109 years ago this week — «El Apóstol» (The Apostle), by Quirino Cristiani, premiered in Argentina. A political satire about an hour long, it is considered the first animated feature film on record.

Continue readingShow less

Cristiani was born in Italy in 1896 and arrived in Argentina aged four. He was called in by the newsreel producer Federico Valle, who was looking for someone to animate caricatures for his newsreels, and spent about a year making the film: 58,000 frames on 35 mm film, projected at 14 per second. The figures were drawn by the caricaturist Diógenes «El Mono» Taborda, and Cristiani, who did practically all the animation by himself, built a machine with cranks, pedals, pulleys and a downward-pointing camera, which he went on to patent. For the climax, the architect Andrés Ducaud made scale models of Buenos Aires buildings.

The story had President Hipólito Yrigoyen ascend to heaven to ask for Jupiter's thunderbolts and cleanse Buenos Aires of corruption — with the result that the city was left in ashes. The film was a success, and Cristiani went on to make «Peludópolis» (1931), presented as the first animated feature film with sound. In 1941 Walt Disney visited Argentina, offered him a job and, according to the Argentine press, was impressed to learn that he had animated everything by himself; Cristiani turned him down.

The ending is bitter: fires in 1957 and 1961 destroyed most of Cristiani's work, including «El Apóstol», of which no copy survives — we know what it was like only from descriptions, photographs and testimony. That is why the earliest animated feature film that can still be seen is «The Adventures of Prince Achmed» (1926), by the German Lotte Reiniger, made with cut-out silhouettes. Cristiani died in 1984 in Bernal, near Buenos Aires, and his name, almost forgotten outside Argentina, has been rescued by historians and by documentaries such as «Sin dejar rastros» (Without a Trace), by Diego Kartaszewicz.
