2027 Budget goes to the vote today with approval all but guaranteed: PS abstains, Chega votes against

Final vote expected this afternoon · PS has announced it will abstain · 343 amendments approved at committee stage (fact) · 24/11/2026

Decision day has arrived for the 2027 State Budget. The final overall vote is scheduled for this Tuesday, 24 November, in the afternoon plenary session of the Assembleia da República, Portugal's parliament, due to start at 14:30 Lisbon time, according to RTP, the public broadcaster. Approval is taken for granted: the PS (Socialist Party) has announced it will abstain, as it did at the first reading, which means the votes of the PSD and CDS, the governing parties, are enough to pass it. Chega votes against, André Ventura confirmed the day before in an interview with TVI/CNN Portugal.

The marathon of article-by-article votes (the so-called committee stage) only ended after midnight: at 00:05 this Tuesday, with 134 proposals approved on the last day alone. In all, the parties tabled around 2,900 amendments — a record number — and 343 passed. The PS got the most approved (108), followed by PSD/CDS (70) and Chega (57), according to the count cited by RTP.

The Budget going to the vote is no longer quite the one the Government submitted. Among the changes approved against the executive's wishes, nearly always with PS and Chega votes: zero VAT on a basket of foods from 1 January to 31 March 2027 (paid for by ending the reduced 10% income tax rate for landlords charging moderate rents), university tuition fees frozen for 2027-2028, PSP and GNR (police and national guard) allowances linked to inflation, and any bonus for pensioners turned into a permanent increase. Also approved were a public guarantee for landlords when rent goes unpaid and an extension of the State guarantee to young people building their first home.

The tone has risen over the past few days. Finance Minister Joaquim Miranda Sarmento accused the PS and Chega of forming a «fatal bloc»; both parties reject the idea. José Luís Carneiro, the PS leader, says the Budget remains «structurally equivalent» in spending and revenue. The President of the Republic, António José Seguro, said he expected «an approved Budget». The result will only be known in the afternoon, after this edition's closing time.

CrowdStrike to buy Zscaler for $43.9 billion, the biggest deal ever in cybersecurity

Deal announced by both companies · regulator and shareholder approval still needed · completion not before the second half of 2027 · 23/11/2026

Two of the world's biggest cybersecurity companies are joining forces. CrowdStrike announced on Monday 23 November that it had agreed to buy Zscaler for around $43.9 billion, paid entirely in shares. Each Zscaler share will be worth 1.135 CrowdStrike shares, a 34% premium on Friday's closing price, according to TheStreet and Yahoo Finance. The deal still needs the green light from regulators and shareholders.

Continue readingShow less

What does each one do? CrowdStrike protects computers, servers and user accounts; it is known, among other things, for the faulty update that knocked out millions of Windows computers in July 2024. Zscaler protects companies' connections to the internet and to their applications, without going through traditional firewalls and virtual private networks (VPNs). With the purchase, CrowdStrike moves into network security, which it describes as the biggest market in the sector. «Security used to be about keeping the adversary out. Today, you need security to let AI in», said chief executive George Kurtz, quoted by PYMNTS.

The stock market didn't like all of it. CrowdStrike shares fell by between 9% and 12% at one point during the session, while Zscaler's rose more than 13%. CrowdStrike's existing shareholders will own around 84% of the combined company. Zscaler's founder, Jay Chaudhry, who together with people linked to him holds about 34% of the company, has already committed to backing the deal and, according to Yahoo Finance, will continue to lead Zscaler and join CrowdStrike's board.

What comes next? The companies expect to add around $1 billion a year in revenue in the first 30 months after the purchase and to save $500 million in costs. The completion date varies depending on the source: mid-2027, according to Yahoo Finance, or between August 2027 and January 2028, according to TheStreet. On the same day, CrowdStrike reported the best quarter in its history, with $419 million in net new annual recurring revenue, up 58% on a year earlier.

Flaw in a Microsoft service used to attack Equinor and another Norwegian energy company; Norway orders all access keys to be replaced

Attacks confirmed by Norway's security authority · Microsoft has already fixed the flaw · extent and perpetrators unknown · 23/11/2026

A serious flaw in a Microsoft cloud service was exploited to try to steal keys and passwords from energy companies in Norway. The Norwegian National Security Authority (NSM) said the problem affected several organisations in the country, among them the oil company Equinor and the power company Skagerak Energi, which had disclosed incidents on 17 and 18 November. According to SecurityWeek and The Register, which reported the case on 23 November, there are also victims outside Norway.

Continue readingShow less

What was involved? Azure API Management, a service companies use to manage and protect the connections between their applications. Microsoft says the flaw only affects «Classic» versions of the service set up without following its security recommendations, or that stored secrets directly in the service instead of in a dedicated vault. Access passwords, subscription keys and management credentials could leak from there. Microsoft detected the problem on 18 November and applied an automatic fix the next day.

Was there any damage? Equinor says it has no evidence that data was stolen or altered. Skagerak Energi also found no sign of compromised information and says production was not affected. It is not known who is behind the attacks, nor how many companies were hit. Microsoft has not publicly released an identification number or a severity score for the flaw.

The NSM's advice is clear: treat all keys and passwords stored in the service as stolen and replace them. The fix closes the door, but does not cancel credentials that may already have been taken, and these could open other systems connected to the service, warned an expert quoted by SecurityWeek. The NSM praised Equinor for spotting the problem early and sharing the information.

Guterres gives the world a year to agree common rules against the risks of AI, with independent oversight

Appeal by the UN Secretary-General on Monday, in Barbados · a proposal, with no agreement between countries · 23/11/2026

One year to set up global rules for artificial intelligence. That is the deadline António Guterres, the UN Secretary-General, proposed on Monday 23 November at the close of the Special Summit on AI Safety in Bridgetown, the capital of Barbados. He asked countries for a multilateral framework to manage the risks of AI, with independent oversight, according to AFP and the Chinese news agency Xinhua.

Continue readingShow less

Where to start? Guterres suggested three starting points: shared safety measures, a testing regime for the most powerful systems and minimum requirements for those who oversee them. He also called for more transparency about serious incidents and «near misses», so that countries can learn from each other. And he said international institutions still reflect the world of 1945.

The summit ended with the «Bridgetown Call», which asks governments, companies and foundations for more investment in AI in the countries of the Global South — including shared regional pools of computing capacity. Barbados's Prime Minister, Mia Mottley, argued that every country should be able to build, test and use AI on its own terms.

What we don't know yet. Guterres is at the end of his term, and his successor takes office in the new year. The sources we consulted do not say which countries support the proposal, and AFP points out that the rivalry between the US and China makes an agreement difficult. For now, it is an appeal — not a commitment.

World's largest trial of AI in breast cancer screening begins in the UK: 700,000 women over three years

Clinical trial began on Monday in Cambridge · results only in years' time · benefits yet to be proven · 23/11/2026

The world's largest study of AI in breast cancer screening has begun in the UK. It is called EDITH, and the first invitations went out on Monday 23 November at Cambridge University Hospitals. Over three years, almost 700,000 women attending routine mammograms on the NHS will be invited to take part, at 30 sites.

Continue readingShow less

What do they want to find out? Two things. First, whether AI helps detect cancers earlier, by flagging areas of the mammogram where a tumour might be missed. Second, whether many mammograms can be read by a single radiologist, instead of the two required today, just as safely. Four AI systems will be tested.

The machines do not decide on their own. Every mammogram will still be seen by at least one specialist, and clinical decisions stay with the doctors. The invitation letters explain how to opt out. The study costs £10 million, paid by the Government through the National Institute for Health and Care Research (NIHR), and is led by the universities of Warwick and Cambridge.

Why does this matter? Around 55,000 breast cancers are diagnosed in the UK each year, and there is a shortage of radiologists. If AI proves at least as safe as the current method, it could free up specialists and shorten waits — but, for now, none of this has been shown.

Mozilla, Shopify and thousands of European companies ask Brussels to force Apple to open up iPhone browsers

Open letter sent to the European Commission · request for formal proceedings · Commission has not yet replied · 23/11/2026

If you use Firefox or Chrome on an iPhone, you are in practice using Apple's engine. A coalition of European companies and organisations sent an open letter on Monday 23 November to the President of the European Commission, Ursula von der Leyen, asking her to open formal proceedings against Apple. The signatories, including Mozilla, Shopify, the Vivaldi browser and the accelerator Y Combinator, say they represent between 45,000 and 55,000 companies and organisations in the EU.

Continue readingShow less

What is the complaint? Since March 2024, the EU's Digital Markets Act (DMA) has required Apple to let other browsers use their own engine, the component that displays pages and decides what websites can do. According to Open Web Advocacy, which organised the letter, more than two and a half years later no browser has managed to move its European users from WebKit, Apple's engine, to its own. Mozilla explained that, to use its engine on iOS, it would have to launch a new Firefox app, and users would have to move their bookmarks, passwords and history over by hand.

Why does this matter? The signatories say these rules prevent web apps from competing with those in the App Store, where Apple charges commission. Apple replies that it requires rival engines to meet privacy and security standards, such as fixing flaws quickly, to protect users. The coalition, which includes newspaper publishers and digital rights groups, stresses that it is not asking for new laws: only for the Commission to enforce the one that already exists.

Continue readingShow less

Lorenzini was the son of a cook and a seamstress who worked for the Ginori family of marquises, who paid for his education. He spent a few years in a seminary but never became a priest: the cause of Italian unification led him into journalism and, in 1848, he volunteered in the First Italian War of Independence. He founded the satirical newspaper «Il Lampione» and wrote for several others. The pen name Collodi comes from the Tuscan village where his mother was born and where he spent his summers as a child. The book is often cited as the most translated non-religious work in the world, in more than 300 languages and dialects. In 1940, Disney turned it into an animated film, and in the village of Collodi there has been a Pinocchio Park since 1956, which is turning 70. Collodi died in 1890, without imagining how far his puppet's nose would go.

Did you know that «Happy Birthday to You», the English version of the birthday song Portuguese speakers know as «Parabéns a Você», had an owner for decades and only became free of copyright in 2016?

Did you know that one of the most sung songs on the planet has earned millions of dollars in copyright fees? We are talking about «Happy Birthday to You», the English version of the birthday song heard at parties all over the world. Until 2016, anyone who wanted to use it in a film, a series or a record in the US had to pay a music publisher, Warner/Chappell.

Continue readingShow less

The story begins in 1893, in Kentucky, in the US. Patty Smith Hill, a kindergarten teacher, and her older sister, Mildred Hill, wrote a simple song for children called «Good Morning to All», published in a songbook for pre-school. The tune was easy to sing and it spread. Later, someone swapped «good morning» for «happy birthday» and the birthday version was born, which at first was just an optional verse.

In 1935, a publisher, Clayton F. Summy, registered the copyright of «Happy Birthday to You». The company was bought in 1988 by Warner/Chappell, for around $25 million, according to court records. From then on, the publisher charged for commercial use of the song: it is estimated to have received around $2 million a year. Singing happy birthday at home was never a problem, but for many years films and restaurants preferred to make up other songs so as not to pay. Warner expected to hold the rights until 2030.

The turning point came from a film-maker. Jennifer Nelson was making a documentary about the song, paid $1,500 to use it and decided to go to court. In September 2015, Judge George H. King, in Los Angeles, concluded that the 1935 registration covered only a piano arrangement and not the lyrics. Warner/Chappell agreed to pay back $14 million to those who had paid for licences, and in June 2016 the judge approved the settlement that put the song in the public domain. In other words: since then, happy birthday really does belong to everyone.

Walk&Talk and Anda&Fala: for 15 years, a project born out of street art has been bringing artists from all over the world to São Miguel and backing young Azorean creators

Origins

In 2011, a street art festival began painting walls in Ponta Delgada, in the Azores. It was called Walk&Talk and, 15 years later, it is a contemporary arts biennial with artists from all over the world, organised by the Azorean association Anda&Fala. Until Saturday 28 November, the association's space in Ponta Delgada, vaga, is showing the work of three young Azorean artists who won awards this year.

Continue readingShow less

The idea came from the curator Jesse James, who grew up on São Miguel. According to The Art Newspaper, Walk&Talk began as a short summer celebration of street art. Over the years, it added exhibitions, performance, music and residencies, and twelve editions of the festival brought together more than 500 artists, curators and creators, according to the announcement of the first biennial. In 2023 the shift to a biennial began, with residencies and preparatory work in the years without a festival. The first Walk&Talk Biennial, «Gestos de Abundância» (Gestures of Abundance), ran from 25 September to 30 November 2025, with more than 80 artists in nine venues across the island — from a former Franciscan convent and a 19th-century market to a pineapple plantation and the Arquipélago, the contemporary arts centre housed in a former factory in Ribeira Grande.

The aim is to see the Azores not as a periphery but as a meeting point in the middle of the Atlantic. Anda&Fala, a non-profit association, says it wants to «promote new centralities for contemporary creation». As well as the biennial, it runs vaga, an art space in Ponta Delgada, and the Prémio Nova Vaga (New Wave Prize), which every two years awards €5,000 creation grants to artists aged 18 to 35 who were born in and live in the Azores. This year, the winners were André Carreiro Oliveira, from Faial, the collective Atelineiras, from São Miguel, and Tomás Toste, from Terceira, with João Francisco Reis as curator.

Why does it matter? In an archipelago far from the major centres, the project has Azorean artists working side by side with international names and takes art to schools, markets and farmland. And it keeps growing: in January, the association joined the European project TIDAL ArtS, which brings together art and science to protect oceans and rivers and will bring an artist residency to São Miguel, with a public presentation scheduled for the 2027 biennial. All this in a year in which Ponta Delgada is Portuguese Capital of Culture.
